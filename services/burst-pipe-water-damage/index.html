Signs of a Hidden Burst Inside a Wall

A pipe doesn't always burst where you can see it. Watch for:

  • A sudden drop in water pressure
  • Your water meter still spinning with every tap off
  • A higher than normal water bill
  • Wet drywall, peeling paint, or a bulge in the wall
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Warped baseboards or damp carpet along a wall

How We Restore Burst Pipe Damage

  1. Extract standing water from floors and carpet
  2. Use thermal imaging to track water inside walls and ceilings
  3. Remove baseboards and drill small holes to dry wall cavities where needed
  4. Set up air movers and dehumidifiers
  5. Check moisture daily and log the readings
  6. Replace drywall, flooring, and trim that couldn't be saved

Frequently Asked Questions

Is burst pipe damage covered by insurance?

A sudden burst is often covered. Damage from a slow leak or a lack of maintenance often isn't. See our insurance guide.

Do you fix the pipe?

A licensed plumber repairs the pipe. We handle the water removal, drying, and restoration.

Why does the ceiling below the leak look worse than the leak area?

Water follows gravity. It collects on the ceiling below and soaks the drywall there.

How long does it take to dry after a burst pipe?

Usually 3 to 5 days. Wall cavities and hardwood can take longer.
