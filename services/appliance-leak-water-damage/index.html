Why Appliance Leaks Get Worse Than They Look

Most appliances sit on the floor with cabinets or walls around them. Water runs under the appliance, into the gap below the cabinets, and soaks into the subfloor and drywall. By the time you see water on the floor, much more has spread out of sight. Particleboard cabinet bases swell quickly and often can't be saved.

What We Do

  1. Pull the appliance out and check the space behind and below it
  2. Extract water from the floor and under cabinets
  3. Use moisture meters on subfloor, cabinet bases, and walls
  4. Remove toe kicks to dry under cabinets without tearing them out when possible
  5. Dry the area with air movers and dehumidifiers
  6. Replace flooring, drywall, or cabinet parts that are too damaged

Clean or Dirty Water?

  • Supply line leak (hose, ice maker line, inlet valve): clean water
  • Drain side leak (washer or dishwasher drain water): gray water with soap and food, which needs cleaning and sanitizing, not just drying

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you save my kitchen cabinets?

Often yes, if we dry them quickly. Solid wood cabinets hold up well. Particleboard bases may need replacing.

How often should washer hoses be replaced?

Rubber hoses are commonly replaced every 5 years. Braided steel hoses last longer and are less likely to burst.

Is appliance leak damage covered by insurance?

A sudden burst is often covered. A slow drip that went unnoticed for a long time often isn't.

Should I keep using the appliance?

No. Turn off its water supply and wait until the leak is repaired.
