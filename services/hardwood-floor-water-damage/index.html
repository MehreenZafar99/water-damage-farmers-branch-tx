Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Farmers Branch

Wood soaks up water and swells. Left alone, a wet hardwood floor can cup, buckle, and pull away from the subfloor. The good news is that fast drying can save many wood floors. We dry hardwood floors in Farmers Branch homes with equipment made for dense wood.

Why Wood Floors Need Special Drying

Hardwood is a Class 4 material. It's dense and gives up water slowly. Regular air movers dry the surface too fast while the bottom stays wet, which makes cupping worse. We use a few tools built for wood:

  • Floor drying mats that pull moisture up through the boards
  • Dehumidifiers set to dry the air without shocking the wood
  • Moisture meters that read the wood and subfloor separately
  • Subfloor drying from below when there's access

Types of Wood Flooring We Dry

  • Solid hardwood nailed to a wood subfloor
  • Engineered hardwood glued down to a concrete slab
  • Engineered floating floors
  • Wood look laminate, which often can't be saved once it swells

Most Farmers Branch homes sit on a concrete slab, so glued down engineered wood is common. Water under glued floors can break the bond, which is why slab moisture readings matter.

Don't Rush the Refinish

Many cupped floors flatten as they dry. If a floor is sanded while still wet, it can crown once it finishes drying. We recommend waiting until readings confirm the wood is back to normal before any sanding or refinishing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does hardwood take to dry?

Often 1 to 3 weeks, depending on how wet it got and the type of floor.

Will my cupped floor go back to flat?

Many do once fully dried. Some need light sanding after drying is complete.

Can laminate flooring be dried?

Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells and doesn't recover. It usually needs replacing once swollen.

Should I pull up the boards myself?

No. Removing boards can cause more damage and may affect your insurance claim. Let us assess first.
