The Drought and Rain Cycle

North Texas clay soil acts almost like a living thing under your home.

In a dry summer, the clay shrinks and pulls away from the foundation. Parts of the slab can settle.

When heavy rain returns, the clay soaks up water and swells again. The slab gets pushed back up.

Over the years, this back and forth flexes the slab and the pipes inside it. Joints loosen, pipes rub against concrete, and small leaks start.

That's why slab leaks are one of the most common water damage calls in Farmers Branch.

What Happens After a Slab Leak Is Found

  1. A leak detection plumber finds and repairs the line
  2. We measure moisture in the slab, flooring, walls, and cabinets
  3. Wet pad, swollen laminate, and damaged baseboards are removed
  4. The slab and structure are dried with targeted equipment
  5. We test the slab before new flooring goes back down

Concrete gives up water slowly, so slab drying often takes longer than a normal leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I prevent slab leaks?

You can lower the risk. Keeping soil moisture steady around the foundation in dry months helps reduce movement. A plumber can also test your lines if you suspect a leak.

Does a slab leak mean foundation damage?

Not always. But water under the slab can make the soil swell or wash out, so fixing it quickly matters.

How do you dry water inside concrete?

With targeted air movers, dehumidifiers, and sometimes heat, while testing the slab until readings are normal.

Can I put new flooring down right after the leak is fixed?

Wait until the slab tests dry. Flooring over a wet slab can warp or grow mold.
