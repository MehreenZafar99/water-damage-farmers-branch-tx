How We Find Water Without Tearing Walls Apart

Thermal imaging camera. Wet drywall shows up cooler than dry drywall. We can see the shape of the wet area through the wall.

Pin and pinless moisture meters. These confirm how wet the drywall and framing really are.

Hygrometer readings. Humidity inside a wall cavity tells us if water is still active.

Small inspection holes. When needed, a hole smaller than a coin behind the baseboard lets us check inside.

Once we know exactly where the water is, we open only what's necessary. Often that means removing baseboards and drilling small holes to dry the wall in place instead of cutting out large sections.

Why Older Plumbing Leaks Inside Walls

Homes that have been around for decades may still have original pipes. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, copper can develop pinhole leaks, and fittings loosen over time. Because these pipes run inside walls, the first sign of trouble is usually damage, not water.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you find the leak or fix the pipe?

We find where the water spread and dry it. A licensed plumber repairs the pipe. We can pinpoint the wet area to help the plumber open the right spot.

Will you have to replace my whole wall?

Rarely. Most walls can be dried in place with small holes behind the baseboard. We only remove drywall that is damaged or growing mold.

How long can a hidden leak go unnoticed?

Weeks or even months. That's why musty smells and water bill jumps are worth checking.

Is a hidden leak covered by insurance?

Policies often exclude long term leaks. Finding and stopping them fast gives you the best chance.
