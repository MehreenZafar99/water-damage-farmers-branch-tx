## Your Freeze Plan: Before, During, and After

### Before the Freeze
- Disconnect garden hoses and cover outdoor faucets
- Shut off and drain the sprinkler system
- Wrap exposed pipes in the garage and attic with foam sleeves
- Find your main water shutoff and make sure it turns
- Keep a flashlight and towels where you can grab them

### During the Freeze
- Let faucets on exterior walls drip, both hot and cold
- Open cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes
- Keep the thermostat steady, day and night
- Keep the garage door closed
- If a faucet stops flowing, a pipe may be frozen. Warm it gently with a hair dryer, never an open flame

### After the Freeze
- Check your water meter with every tap off. If it moves, there's a leak
- Look under sinks, in the garage, and in the attic for water
- Watch ceilings for new stains over the next day or two
- If a pipe split, shut off the main valve and call a plumber and us

## Why Burst Pipes Show Up Late

A frozen pipe often cracks while it's full of ice, but the ice plugs the crack. The water only starts spraying once it thaws, sometimes a day after temperatures rise. Many Farmers Branch homeowners found their leaks this way after the February 2021 winter storm.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which pipes in my home are most likely to freeze?
Pipes in exterior walls, the attic, the garage, and outdoor faucets and sprinkler lines.

### Should I shut off my water if I leave during a freeze?
Yes. Turn off the main valve and open a faucet to drain the lines. That lowers the risk of a flood while you're away.

### Can you start drying before the plumber fixes the pipe?
Yes, as long as the water is shut off. Starting early limits the damage.

### Is freeze damage covered by insurance?
Often, if the home was heated and you took reasonable care. Check your policy for details.
