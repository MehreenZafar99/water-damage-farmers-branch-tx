## Why Leaks Spread Further Here

Mercer Crossing has thousands of apartments and many attached townhomes. In homes that share walls, floors, and ceilings, water doesn't stay put.

- A washer leak on the third floor can reach the unit below within minutes
- A burst line inside a shared wall can wet two homes at once
- A water heater in an upstairs closet drains down through the floor system
- A single sprinkler head in an apartment building can flood a hallway and several units

That's why we check the rooms next to and below the leak, not just the room where you found it.

## Newer Homes Still Leak

Most of Mercer Crossing is new construction, but newer doesn't mean leak proof. The problems we see here are usually installation and appliance related:

- Loose or failed PEX fittings
- Washer and ice maker supply lines
- Clogged AC condensate lines in summer
- Shower pans and tub drains leaking into the ceiling below
- Water heater drain pans that overflow

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My neighbor's leak damaged my unit. Who pays?
It depends on the cause and the policies involved. Usually each owner's or renter's policy covers their own property, and the building policy covers shared parts. Document everything and tell your insurer.

### Do you work inside apartment buildings?
Yes. Portable extractors and drying equipment let us work in upper floor units and hallways.

### Can you dry my unit while I still live there?
In most cases, yes. The equipment is noisy but safe. We set it up to keep a path through your home.

### Do I need to wait for my HOA before you start?
No. Start drying right away to stop the damage. Let your HOA know at the same time.
