## Floor by Floor: Where Leaks Start and Where They Show Up

| Floor | Common leak sources | Where you'll see the damage |
|---|---|---|
| Top floor | Upstairs laundry, primary bath shower, AC unit in the attic, roof | Ceiling of the floor below, walls near the laundry closet |
| Middle floor | Kitchen sink, dishwasher, fridge ice maker, powder room | Ceiling of the ground floor or garage, kitchen cabinets |
| Ground floor | Water heater, slab plumbing, exterior doors in heavy rain | Flooring, baseboards, garage walls |

The key point: the stain you see is usually one floor below the real problem.

---

## Upstairs Laundry Is the Top Risk

Many townhomes put the washer and dryer on the upper floor near the bedrooms. It's convenient, but a burst washer hose there can release water fast, right above your living room or kitchen.

**Simple ways to lower the risk:**
- Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel hoses
- Make sure a drain pan sits under the washer
- Don't run the washer while no one is home
- Know where the washer's shutoff valves are

## How We Dry a Multi Story Home

1. Find the source and every floor it reached
2. Extract water from each level, starting at the top
3. Check subfloor and ceiling cavities between floors with thermal imaging
4. Open small ceiling sections only where water is trapped
5. Dry each level with its own air movers and dehumidifiers
6. Repair ceilings, drywall, and flooring floor by floor

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my ground floor ceiling wet when nothing leaked there?
Water from the floor above collects on top of your ceiling drywall. The source is almost always one level up.

### Will you need to cut open my ceiling?
Sometimes a small section, if water is trapped between floors. We use thermal imaging first so any openings are as small as possible.

### Does my HOA cover leaks inside my townhome?
Usually the HOA covers shared parts like the roof or exterior. Leaks from your own plumbing or appliances are usually on your policy. Check your HOA documents.

### How long does drying take in a townhome?
Usually 3 to 5 days. Drying between floors can take a bit longer.
